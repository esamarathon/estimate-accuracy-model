3 · What the data says

  • Estimates are honest — trust them. The median run finishes 2:25 under its estimate; only a third run over at all. The submitted estimate is the best single predictor of run time we have. Estimates have tightened over the years (S22 median ratio 0.938 → W26 0.982), so the old sandbag buffer is eroding.
  • The speeches are the systematic error. Intro + outro add a median 2:34 (p80 3:54) to every slot, independent of run length. That flat tax flips the picture: 53% of slots exceed the estimate even though most runs don't.
  • The tail is asymmetric. A blown no-reset run loses 10–19 minutes (p95); a great run saves only a few. Short runs (<20m) carry the worst relative risk (p80 ratio 1.27); >2h runs the worst absolute risk (p80 +9m).
  • Setup, not estimates, drains the schedule. 60% of setups exceed the booked 10 minutes (median 11:26, p80 17:09) — +10.2h of unbooked time across two events, an order of magnitude more than net estimate drift.
  • Runner history is a dead end. 24 runners ran in both W26 and S25; the correlation of their over/under ratio between events is −0.12. Per-runner bias is noise — don't build that feature.
  • Days self-balance — mostly. Whole-event drift was −1.2h (S22), −0.0h (S25), +2.4h (W26): long-run padding roughly cancels the speech tax at day scale. W26's +2.4h shows the marge is gone as estimates tighten.

4 · The predictor

Predicted slot (what a run actually occupies between setups), from the submitted estimate:

speech      = 2:30                        # median; use 4:00 for p80 planning
run_p50     = estimate × r50(bucket)      # see table — ≈ estimate
slot_p50    = run_p50 + speech            # honest booking
slot_p80    = estimate + 5 min            # <2h runs — flat!
            = estimate + 9 min            # >2h runs
setup_p50   = 11:30                       # book 15:00, not 10:00
flag_risk   = estimate < 20 min           # worst relative spread (p80 ×1.27)
            ∨ estimate > 2 h              # worst absolute tail (+9m)

Fitted on the 379 unified slot rows; the r-table and the flat "+5 min" rule agree — use whichever is easier to implement. A day scheduled at slot_p50 per run + 15-minute setups reproduces the historical day totals within ±1%.

5 · Method & caveats

  • Truth sources. ESA run-timing sheets for W26 + S22 (timer start/stop, intro/outro seconds, setup); YouTube VOD cut durations for S25 and W26. On the 94 W26 runs covered by both, VOD length equals sheet slot time with median 0s error (p10 −2s, p90 +1s) — the cuts are frame-exact slot captures, so S25's VOD numbers are used as real slot times.
  • Archived Horaro is NOT ground truth: 92–94% of its "final" lengths still equal the submitted estimate — ESA amends start-time drift live, not per-run lengths. Any analysis regressed on it would conclude estimates are near-perfect.
  • Matching. Oengus estimates joined to schedule rows by normalized game + category + runner (87% S25, 77% W26 match rate); the timing sheets carry their own estimate column, used directly. Showcase/speech rows excluded.
  • Speech times for S25 are inside its slot numbers but not separable (no sheet); the split relies on W26 + S22.
  • Survivorship: only runs that made the schedule are observable — this predicts scheduled-run behavior, which is exactly the scheduling use case.
